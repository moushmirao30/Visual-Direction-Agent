AURU BRAND RESEARCH DOCUMENT
Source: Visual Direction Research Exercise + Editorial Project Brief
Category: Brand Research — Luxury Wellness

================================================================================
BRAND OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Brand Name: AURU
Tagline: Evidence, Elevated.
Category: Minimal luxury wellness — supplements, adaptogens, functional beauty
Market Space: Premium wellness with scientific credibility and emotional calm
Essence: Where peer-reviewed science meets sensory calm — rituals that feel indulgent and perform on paper.
Founded: 2024 | Origin: Physician-designer collaboration, Copenhagen and Kyoto

Core Promise: Results you can measure, rituals you want to keep.
Brand Summary: Science-backed stillness for modern bodies. "Quiet minds are the new luxury."

================================================================================
TARGET AUDIENCE
================================================================================

Primary Age Range: 28–48
Profile: Urban, design-literate professionals — creatives, founders, professionals
Psychographics: Values calm mastery, credible data, crafted materials, and time well spent
Behaviours: Reads ingredients before buying. Values quality over quantity. Prefers subtle luxury. Feels overwhelmed by wellness marketing. Seeks routines over quick fixes.
Key Mindset Quote: "I want products that work without demanding attention."
Rejects: Novelty churn, neon promises, performative wellness, trend-driven visuals

================================================================================
BRAND POSITIONING
================================================================================

Positioning Statement: AURU delivers clinically validated outcomes through sensorially refined rituals.
Differentiators: Peer-reviewed formulas, material warmth, editorial restraint, measurable progress
Landscape Position: Between lab-grade efficacy and boutique hospitality
White Space: Evidence-forward wellness with tactile, humane design

Key Tension: Translating scientific rigour into an experience that soothes — never sterile, never loud.
The Gap: Clinical efficacy often feels cold; luxury wellness often lacks proof.
Why Now: A post-peak biohacking world seeking grounded, sustainable care and fewer but better rituals.

Pull Quote: "Give me what works, then get out of the way."

================================================================================
BRAND VOICE
================================================================================

Tone: Calm, precise, warm
Voice Characteristics: Measured, warm, refined, intelligent, minimal
Language Principles: Say less. Lead with clarity. Evidence first, flourish second.
Sounds Like: Composed, intentional, unhurried
Not: Hypey, dense, or clinical jargon

Do: "Designed for everyday ritual." / "We value consistency over intensity."
Don't: "Transform your life" / "Unlock your best self" / No exclamation marks

Sample Copy: "One capsule, morning. 8 weeks to steadier energy. No noise. Just data — and a softer landing."

================================================================================
VISUAL DIRECTION
================================================================================

Design Principles: Spacious grids, thin rules, generous margins
Materials Feel: Matte paper grain, soft shadows, brushed stone accents
Mood Reference: Morning light on limestone, linen, and glass. Hands, not faces. Breath and texture over spectacle.
Photography Direction: Hands, product on surface, close-up texture — no faces, no energy, no motion

Colour Palette:
- #F5F1E8 — Warm Cream (background base)
- #D8C7AE — Stone/Sand (structure colour)
- #9B8C7D — Taupe (depth colour)
- #6F7563 — Sage Grey-Green (secondary structure)
- #2B2B2B — Deep Charcoal (type, near-black)

Typography System:
- Display/Heading: Cormorant Garamond or DM Serif Display (editorial, considered)
- Body/Label: DM Sans or Inter (clean, readable)
- Usage Hierarchy: Heading / Subheading / Body / Label / Caption
- Show at 3 sizes minimum
- Tracking: generous on headings, tight on body

Icons: Minimal line marks for dosing, time, and progress

================================================================================
THEME ANALYSIS — RESEARCH FINDINGS
================================================================================

Three aesthetic themes were evaluated:

THEME 1 — COLD MINERAL ("Aesop-coded")
Description: Clinical, mineral-toned aesthetic inspired by apothecary branding
Rejection Reason: Felt overly medicated and chemical rather than natural
User Response: "Much clinical-based designs. Feels too medicated — less natural, more chemical."

THEME 2 — WARM BOTANICALS ("Generic Wellness")
Description: Warm, nature-forward aesthetic typical of wellness branding
Rejection Reason: Visual clutter and generic, overcrowded feel
User Response: "Warm but feels like too much noise in the background. Demands obvious attention."

THEME 3 — MODERN VOID ("Dark Luxury") — SELECTED
Description: Dark, restrained aesthetic built on negative space and minimal visual cues
Selection Reason: Aligned with "Quiet Luxury" positioning — less noise, more sophisticated
User Response: "Selected — fits the quote 'Quiet Luxury' — less noise, more sophisticated vibe."

================================================================================
RESEARCH INSIGHT SUMMARY
================================================================================

Critical Findings:
1. Users are sensitive to the fine line between "natural" and "clinical" — Theme 1 crossed it
2. Warmth alone does not signal premium; Theme 2's botanical approach read as generic
3. Visual restraint is the strongest signal of sophistication in this category
4. The "Quiet Luxury" principle — communicating quality through absence rather than excess — resonated most strongly

Key Insight: Users associate luxury with what is NOT shown rather than what is.
Overt wellness cues (botanicals, warmth, clutter) reduce perceived exclusivity.
Clinical precision without warmth feels pharmaceutical.
The ideal space sits between: dark, intentional, and unhurried.
