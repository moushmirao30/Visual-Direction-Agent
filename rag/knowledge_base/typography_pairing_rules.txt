TYPOGRAPHY PAIRING LOGIC AND HIERARCHY RULES
Source: Editorial Design Principles, Brand Typography Research — Multi-Aesthetic Reference
Category: Design Theory — Typography

================================================================================
FOUNDATIONAL PAIRING PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

Core rule: pair typefaces that share a quality but differ in classification.
Serif + sans-serif: standard pairing — contrast in classification, harmony in proportion.
Two serifs: too similar unless weight/size contrast is extreme.
Two sans-serifs: works if one is geometric and one is humanist, or if weight contrast is very strong.

Contrast creates hierarchy. Hierarchy creates reading flow.
The eye needs a visual signal to switch between heading and body.

Typeface classifications and their brand signals:

OLD STYLE SERIFS (Garamond, Caslon, EB Garamond, Cormorant)
Signal: Heritage, craft, editorial authority, European luxury, literary timelessness.
Used in: Quiet luxury, heritage brands, literary publishing, fine art.
Stroke character: Diagonal stress, bracketed serifs, moderate thick-thin variation.

TRANSITIONAL SERIFS (Baskerville, Times, Freight Text)
Signal: Institutional authority, journalism, serious intent.
Used in: Newspapers, academic, legal — less common in contemporary brand design.

MODERN/DIDONE SERIFS (Bodoni, Didot, Canela)
Signal: High fashion drama, couture, editorial boldness.
Used in: Vogue, fashion houses, luxury fragrance, editorial magazines.
Caution: Hairline strokes vanish at small sizes — display use only.

HUMANIST SANS-SERIFS (Gill Sans, Optima, Jost, Source Sans)
Signal: Approachable intelligence, warmth with clarity.
Used in: Premium lifestyle, accessible luxury, cultural institutions.

GEOMETRIC SANS-SERIFS (Futura, DM Sans, Inter, Avenir, Circular)
Signal: Modern, functional, Swiss-influenced precision.
Used in: Tech, Scandinavian brands, Muji, Cos, Everlane, most SaaS products.

GROTESQUE SANS-SERIFS (Helvetica, Akzidenz-Grotesk, Neue Haas Grotesk)
Signal: Confident neutrality — the typeface doesn't ask for attention.
Used in: Celine, Calvin Klein, Bloomberg, Helvetica-era Swiss design.

SLAB SERIFS (Rockwell, Clarendon, Plaak, Sentinel)
Signal: Mechanical, confident, American, robust.
Used in: Outdoor brands, craft beer, heritage Americana, editorial magazines.

VARIABLE / EXPRESSIVE TYPE (display faces, custom marks)
Signal: Brand investment, uniqueness, personality at scale.
Used in: Campaigns, packaging heroes, wordmarks — not body text.

================================================================================
TYPOGRAPHY BY AESTHETIC TERRITORY
================================================================================

--- QUIET LUXURY / PREMIUM WELLNESS ---
Display/Heading: Old style serif (Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond) — tracked generously
Body: Geometric sans (DM Sans, Inter) — regular weight, comfortable leading
Label/Detail: Same sans, uppercase with extended tracking (0.1–0.2em)
Rule: Maximum 2 families. Regular and light weights only. Bold almost never.
Example pairing: Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans

--- HIGH FASHION / EDITORIAL ---
Display: Didone serif (Bodoni, Didot, or custom) at large scale — the hairlines are the point
Body: Grotesque or geometric sans (Helvetica Neue, Inter, Akzidenz) — neutral to contrast drama
Rule: High contrast between headline and body is the signature. Size difference is extreme (96px headline / 14px body).
Key detail: All-caps with extreme tracking for section headers
Example pairing: Bodoni* (open source) + Neue Haas Grotesk

--- DARK LUXURY ---
Display: Thin-weight serif or sans — Didot Light, Canela Thin, or bespoke light sans
Body: Regular weight sans — let space do more work than weight
Rule: Lightness of type weight against dark ground creates the tension. Do not use bold.
Example pairing: Canela Light + Inter Regular (both at generous size)

--- BRUTALIST / RAW TECH ---
Typography is confrontational: oversized, tight-tracked, unresolved
Display: Grotesque or grotesk at extreme size — Helvetica Heavy, Akzidenz Bold
Body: Monospace (IBM Plex Mono, Courier) or standard grotesque — signals raw code/terminal
Rule: Functional hierarchy only. No typographic ornament. No serifs.
Key detail: Type can overrun containers, bleed edges, or be cut by the frame — intentionally unfinished
Example: Helvetica Black headers + IBM Plex Mono body

--- TECH / DIGITAL PRODUCT ---
Display: Geometric or humanist sans — Inter, SF Pro, Inter Display
Body: Same family, smaller weight — consistent family throughout
Rule: Single typeface family with multiple weights (not multiple families)
Hierarchy: Size + weight variation only — no contrast between typeface classifications
Example: Inter Display Bold + Inter Regular (single family)
Note: System fonts (SF Pro, Segoe UI) used intentionally can signal native-platform quality, not laziness

--- ARTISAN / HERITAGE CRAFT ---
Display: Slab serif or rough-edge serif (Sentinel, Plaak, Freight Display, or aged custom)
Body: Humanist sans or readable serif at body size
Accent: Hand-lettered elements or script for product names / provenance detail
Rule: Imperfection is intentional. Slightly aged, textured, or hand-influenced type adds authenticity.
Avoid: Clean geometric sans — too digital, loses the craft signal
Example pairing: Sentinel + Source Sans Pro

--- STREETWEAR / YOUTH CULTURE ---
Display: Bold grotesque, condensed sans, or custom wordmarks — Akzidenz-Grotesk Condensed, Franklin Gothic
Body: Regular grotesque or geometric sans
Rule: Legibility at small merch scale is non-negotiable. Test at 12px embroidery and 5cm print.
Key technique: Oversized bold type as graphic element — type IS the design
Example: Franklin Gothic Condensed + DM Sans Regular

--- NORDIC / SCANDINAVIAN FUNCTIONAL ---
Display: Clean geometric sans, lightly tracked — DM Sans, Aktiv Grotesk, Graphik
Body: Same family, regular weight
Rule: Democratic hierarchy — no single element demands too much attention
Avoid: Serifs (reads as English heritage not Nordic), scripts, anything ornate
Example: Aktiv Grotesk Medium + Aktiv Grotesk Regular (single family)

--- CULTURAL INSTITUTION / MUSEUM ---
Display: Mix is acceptable — confident serif (Freight, Publico) for permanent collection gravitas
Body: Humanist sans (Source Sans, Frutiger) for accessibility and informational clarity
Rule: Legibility across ages and contexts is paramount. Type system must work in signage, print, and digital.
Example pairing: Publico + Source Sans Pro

================================================================================
TRACKING, SPACING, AND WEIGHT AS BRAND SIGNALS
================================================================================

GENEROUS TRACKING on display uppercase: 0.05–0.2em
Signal: Premium, considered, unhurried, editorial. Used across quiet luxury and fashion.

TIGHT TRACKING on headlines: -0.02 to -0.04em
Signal: Bold, assertive, modern, tech-forward. Used in SaaS, streetwear, brutalist.

BODY LINE HEIGHT:
- 1.6–1.8x: Quality reading experience. Premium and editorial.
- 1.4–1.5x: Efficient, functional. Tech and informational.
- Under 1.3x: Dense data, technical documentation, not brand-facing.

WEIGHT RANGE SIGNALS:
- Thin + Regular only: Quiet luxury, dark luxury, minimal
- Regular + Medium: Scandinavian, accessible premium, tech
- Regular + Bold: Editorial, craft, mid-market
- Bold + Black: Streetwear, youth, fast food, disruptive branding

================================================================================
TYPOGRAPHIC HIERARCHY — 5-LEVEL SYSTEM
================================================================================

Level 1 — DISPLAY: 48–96px+. Brand statements, hero headlines, campaign lines.
Level 2 — HEADING: 24–42px. Section titles, feature headings.
Level 3 — SUBHEADING: 16–22px. Article subheads, card titles, navigation. Often uppercase + tracked.
Level 4 — BODY: 14–16px. Paragraphs, descriptions. Comfortable line-height (1.6x+).
Level 5 — LABEL/CAPTION: 10–13px. Specs, captions, legal copy. Often uppercase, extended tracking.

================================================================================
COMMON PAIRING MISTAKES
================================================================================

Never: More than 2 typeface families in a brand system (3+ reads as indecision)
Never: Script/handwriting faces in clinical or tech contexts
Never: Two serifs of similar weight at similar sizes (invisible hierarchy)
Never: Tight tracking on lowercase body text (cheap software default appearance)
Never: System fonts as a deliberate choice without understanding the signal they send
Never: Tracking and weight AND size all varying simultaneously with no clear purpose
Avoid: Using a typeface because it is trending — once ubiquitous, it reads as derivative
